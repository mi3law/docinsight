Definitions

Bulk insertion

One entry that inserts at least 150 characters and at least 20 words. The log does not record the clipboard, so DocInsight says "bulk insertion"; a paste is the usual cause.

Legitimate causes: pasting the student's own draft or notes from elsewhere; voice typing; long quotations; templates; Gemini "Help me write" (labelled separately when its signature shows). Text deleted and re-inserted within the internal-move window is not counted.

Bulk-insertion share (paste share)

Characters that arrived in bulk insertions and are still in the final text, divided by the final length. A passage inserted in bulk and then rewritten counts only for what survives of it.

Baseline text

Text that was already there when the history begins: the first revision of a copied, imported or templated document, the snapshot Google starts from when it has consolidated the oldest history, or another account's text before the writer began. It is excluded from bulk-insertion scoring and labelled. A student who makes a copy of a document starts a new history whose first revision holds the whole text.

Session

Edits separated by a gap of more than 5 minutes belong to different sessions (Revision History's rule; Draftback and Grammarly use 10). Google's own editing-session id, which changes when the document is reopened, is a different thing and is shown separately.

Active writing time

The sum of the gaps between the writer's consecutive edits that are 5 minutes or shorter. Time spent reading the document without editing it is not recorded by Google and cannot be counted.

Word

A run of characters without spaces. Counts match Google Docs' own word count closely but not exactly (Docs treats some punctuation and hyphens differently).

Internal move

A deletion followed, in the same session and within 10 minutes, by an insertion of the same text of at least 40 characters elsewhere (cut and paste inside the document, or a paste undone and repeated). The characters keep their identity, so the text still counts as written when it was first written; it is never counted as a bulk insertion.

Granularity

How Google batched this writer's typing into entries: the share of typing entries that hold a single character. At 60% or more the entries are close to keystrokes and the typing-rhythm indicators are computed; below that, gaps between entries are not gaps between keystrokes and those indicators say "insufficient granularity". Measured on every document, because Google's batching rule is not published.

Typing speed

Characters typed in the busiest one-minute window, divided by 5 (words per minute). Bulk insertions and chunks are left out. Reference bands: 30 to 40 WPM is the high-school norm; above 78 is the top tenth of adult transcription typists; above 120 is beyond observed human speed (Dhakal et al., CHI 2018, 168,000 people). Context only: typing bots choose their own speed.

Linearity

Linearity index = edits that start exactly where the same author's previous edit ended, divided by all such edits. Backward jumps = edits that start before that point. People move around the document; a bot that cannot read the document only appends.

A student who planned on paper and types a clean copy looks linear too.

Delayed revision

Delayed share = deletions with at least one whole word between them and the writer's cursor, divided by all deletions. A deletion run is a series of backspaces in one burst; the mean run is the characters deleted per run. Bots only make immediate 1 to 3 character fixes; people go back and rework.

False positives: clean-copy typists.

Pause structure (experimental)

Gaps between the writer's edits in 300 ms bins, the entropy (variety) of those bins and of burst lengths, and the median pause inside a word, before a word and before a sentence. People pause longest at sentence boundaries. Needs keystroke-level entries.

Interval shape (experimental)

The distribution of gaps inside bursts. Human gaps are heavy-tailed (mean about 239 ms, skewness about 2). A program that picks each delay uniformly between two bounds has no tail. Flag: largest gap under 3 times the median, or skewness under 0.5, over 500 or more gaps.

Burstiness (experimental)

Goh and Barabási's burstiness B = (sd − mean) / (sd + mean) of the gaps between edits: above 0 for bursty human activity, at or below 0 for regular or bounded random delays. Memory M is the correlation between consecutive gaps.

Rate stationarity

Typed characters in each two-minute window. Worth a look when speed is implausibly even (coefficient of variation under 0.15 over 10 or more windows) or when one window is more than three times the median. Breaks and distractions explain most variation.

Typo realism (experimental)

Corrected slips: a single-character backspace right after typing at that spot, per 1,000 typed characters. People make and fix slips constantly; zero over 2,000 or more characters is unusual. Careful writers exist. Surviving spelling errors are not checked in v1.

Formatting order

The share of formatting-only edits made after the writer's last text insertion. Text inserted in bulk or typed by a program tends to be formatted afterwards. Many students format at the end.

Session structure

Sessions, Google's session ids, calendar span, longest session, sessions longer than a class period (60 minutes), and edits between midnight and 5 am. Deadline-night writing is common and not a sign of anything alone.

Letter-pair timing (experimental)

Gaps between consecutive single letters inside a burst, split by whether the two letters are typed with the same hand on a QWERTY keyboard. People type alternating-hand pairs 30 to 80 ms faster; a program's delays do not depend on the letters. Needs keystroke-level entries.

Input-source labels

Gemini "Help me write": an entry whose style operations carry a spellcheck-reset type plus an anchor type (GPTZero's heuristic). Dictation-like: many sentence-sized entries at speech pace with few deletions. Suggestion accept: the same text deleted and re-inserted in one entry. Restore: Google's "Restore this version". These are labels, not flags, and may change with Docs releases.

What this record is

This record describes the writing process as recorded by Google Docs' revision history, read with the teacher's own access. It shows when text arrived and in what amounts; it does not show who typed it, what was on the student's screen, or where text inserted in bulk came from, and it makes no judgement about whether any text was written with artificial intelligence. It is a starting point for a conversation with the student, not a finding.

Legitimate causes of a large single insertion:

  • Pasting from the student's own earlier draft, notes or another Doc: One big paste, often early in a session, sometimes followed by heavy editing.
  • Voice typing (Docs' Tools > Voice typing, Chromebook dictation): Sentence-sized insertions at speech pace; labelled "dictation-like" when the pattern shows.
  • Gemini "Help me write" inside Docs, if the school enables it: A bulk insertion by the student's own account; labelled when its signature shows.
  • Smart Compose and autocorrect: A few words at a time: below any bulk threshold.
  • Phone or tablet Docs app (glide typing, autocorrect): Whole words at a time; different timing from a keyboard.
  • Input method editors (composed text in other scripts): Composed text committed in chunks.
  • Importing a .docx, "Make a copy", templates, Translate document: The whole text in the first revision: labelled baseline text.
  • Accepting a suggested edit: The same text deleted and re-inserted: not counted.
  • Restoring an earlier version: Labelled restore.
  • A collaborator or the teacher editing: Another account: see Authors.
